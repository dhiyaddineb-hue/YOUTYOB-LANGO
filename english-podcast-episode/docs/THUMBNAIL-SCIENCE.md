# Thumbnail science used by the CTR lab

The lab is not a random score. It encodes 2025–2026 packaging research used by serious channels.

## What “good CTR” means

| CTR | Reading |
| --- | --- |
| < 2% | Packaging is the bottleneck |
| 2–4% | Weak for most niches |
| 4–8% | Healthy, especially education |
| > 8% | Protect the pattern, then A/B |

Education sits lower than entertainment. A predicted 6.5% for an A2 English lesson is a strong plate.

## Signals the engine scores

1. **One dominant face or object** — faces win the first 200ms
2. **One clear emotion** — curiosity, delight, authority. Neutral faces die
3. **Contrast** — luminance std + complementary pair (teal/mustard, yellow/navy)
4. **Text discipline** — 1–4 words, not the title repeated
5. **Curiosity gap** — show the reaction, not the answer
6. **Title combo** — thumbnail opens a loop, title gives context
7. **Mobile postage-stamp test** — text still reads at ~160px wide
8. **Safe zone** — keep type off the duration badge (bottom-right)
9. **Clutter** — edge density penalty
10. **Brand lock** — cream / teal / mustard / charcoal

## Ethical rule

A curiosity gap that the video does not close is clickbait. It burns CTR over time. The lab flags overlays that promise a trick the episode does not teach.

## History

Every analysis is appended to `data/thumbnail-history.json` and mirrored in `localStorage` so variants can be compared weeks later.
